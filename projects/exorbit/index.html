Overview

The idea

Exorbit started as a way to see the orbital physics behind real satellite missions. It grew into a game: you fly a small satellite through a field of planets, and their gravity does most of the work.

Point and the satellite turns, hold to fire the engine, and get close to a planet to be pulled in. Circle it and you're in orbit, collecting data that turns into fuel. Spin too hard and the heat climbs. Run out of fuel and the mission is over.

Trajectories come from Newtonian gravity and numerical integration in a custom physics engine, so ellipses, slingshots and escapes behave like the real thing.

Features

What it does

  • Steer and thrustPoint to turn the satellite, hold to fire the engine.
  • Gravity you can feelPlanets pull like magnets; circle one and you are orbiting.
  • Data → fuelOrbiting collects glowing data, which converts into fuel automatically.
  • HeatSpinning too much overheats the satellite.
  • Levels and free roamMission levels with objectives and rewards, or explore freely.
  • UpgradesCoins buy a bigger fuel tank, more efficient thrust and better data collection.
  • Mission guideA nine-step tutorial that teaches you to fly.
  • Minimap and wave modesTrack planets around you and switch data-collection modes.
  • SoundBuilt with the Web Audio API.

Under the hood

The maths

Gravitational forceF = G · m1 · m2 / r²
Orbital velocityv = √(GM / r)
Escape velocityve = √(2GM / r)
Orbital periodT = 2π · √(a³ / GM)

Position and velocity are stepped forward with numerical integration every frame.
